# Prompt templates

Use these templates as structural checklists. Replace brackets with request-specific facts and describe the final image directly rather than exposing template syntax.

## Image 1 — confrontation

Create a 16:9 cinematic, photorealistic [wildlife/documentary/real-world] photograph of [WEAK SUBJECT] confronting [STRONG SUBJECT] in [SETTING]. The power imbalance must be immediately legible: [STRONG SUBJECT] is physically dominant and visually imposing, while [WEAK SUBJECT] is much smaller and tactically disadvantaged but does not flee. Show both subjects with coherent anatomy and believable relative scale. They face one another across a clear confrontation axis, not moving in the same direction. Use [CAMERA HEIGHT AND SIDE], environmental scale cues, natural depth, physically consistent shadows, restrained filmic color, realistic surface detail, and photographic dynamic range. Preserve credible behavior and ecology. No text, UI, watermark, illustration, anime, oil painting, glossy CGI, excessive HDR, duplicated anatomy, or graphic gore.

## Image 2 — defiant weak subject

Using image 1 as the canonical identity and continuity reference, create a tighter three-quarter or medium-close photograph of the exact same [WEAK SUBJECT] in the exact same [SETTING], moments later. Preserve its species, anatomy, proportions, markings, color, age cues, injuries/accessories, terrain, weather, palette, light direction, and confrontation axis. The camera is positioned near the strong opponent’s side and looks toward the weak subject. Keep the strong opponent outside the frame by default; the weak subject looks toward its established off-screen position.

Translate the supplied pose reference semantically only: low center of gravity, support limbs planted firmly, body held against the threat, head steady or slightly lifted, eyes fixed with fear under control, fatigue, anger, and unwavering resolve—weak but not soft, cornered but refusing to retreat. Keep the expression species-correct and the mouth closed unless explicitly requested otherwise. Show enough body to read weight distribution. Do not copy the reference image’s animal identity, coat color, background, phone interface, captions, watermark, or social-media elements. No human facial expressions, superhero pose, comedy, fleeing posture, background replacement, mirrored direction, plastic CGI, painting, or overprocessed skin/fur.

## Pose semantics from the provided cat reference

Use only these transferable cues:

- broad, planted stance;
- body slightly lowered and angled rather than squarely posed;
- head oriented toward the threat/camera;
- narrowed, unwavering gaze;
- visible tension without open-mouth roaring;
- vulnerability remains visible beneath the resolve.

Map the cues to the target species’ real anatomy. For birds use feet, wing carriage, neck, and feather tension; for marine animals use body pitch, fins, tail, and gaze; for insects or microorganisms use species-plausible orientation and appendage tension; for machines use stance, heading, damage state, and lighting rather than facial anthropomorphism.

## Continuity negatives

Reject: species drift, changed markings, changed scale, new accessories, invented injuries, new season or weather, shifted time of day, reversed key light, left-right flip, opponent-direction flip, unrelated background, altered camera universe, duplicated limbs, floating contact, implausible perspective, copied UI/text/logo, cartoon anger marks, human eyebrows, smiling, roaring by default, heroic victory pose, or removal of all visible vulnerability.
